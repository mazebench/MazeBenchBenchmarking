You are playing Slotski, one sliding-block puzzle on a 4-column × 5-row board.

Each solid rectangular block has a permanent uppercase label A–Z. Repeated copies of a letter on the ASCII board are cells of the SAME block. A is the 2×2 target panel. A move chooses ONE block and shifts the entire block ONE cell up, down, left, or right. Blocks do not rotate, push one another, overlap, or slide automatically. The board edge blocks movement. Coordinates are zero-based: x goes right, y goes down.

The exit is the two cells marked vv below the bottom-center columns. Win by moving A downward until it occupies the bottom-center 2×2 area: its top-left coordinate must be (1, 3). The harness declares won immediately at that position; no extra off-board move is required. All other blocks must remain inside the board. There is one level and no level selection or skipping.

Use direct maze_action calls for one move, for example {"action":"block A move up"}, {"action":"AU"}, or {"action":"a up"}. The first letter identifies the block; U, D, L, R identifies the direction. Case does not matter. A blocked move still consumes one action. Undo restores the previous changed move. Reset restores the initial board and clears undo history. Undo and reset each consume one action; neither refunds actions.

Use maze_sequence for multiple moves: {"sequence":"AU BD CL"}, {"sequence":"a up, b down c left"}, {"sequence":"AU3"}, or {"sequence":"a up 3 times"}. An actions array is also accepted, for example {"actions":["AU3","BD","CL"]}. Repeats expand into separate ONE-cell moves. Sequences contain 1–1000 expanded actions. Every step is counted and recorded, including blocked steps. Invalid commands or unknown block labels reject the whole sequence before any move is made. Valid sequences stop only at victory or the action limit; inspect the returned steps for blocked moves.

maze_observe returns the current labelled board, block positions and sizes, target and exit, accepted action counter, status, and record index. It costs no actions. Use its record argument to inspect your own history. move_history/move_0.txt is the initial board, and move_history/move_N.txt is the board after accepted action N. moves.txt and history.jsonl contain your complete action history. Each action reports animation.frame_count and animation.index_record. Read that index, then its listed frame paths to see the before and after boards. These one-cell moves have no hidden intermediate game states. Reading history never changes the board or novelty.

Use only direct maze_observe, maze_action, maze_sequence, and python_exec if explicitly available. Never orchestrate game tools from programs, loops, callbacks, batch executors, or other tools. Only maze_action and maze_sequence can change the game. Do not seek original files, precomputed solutions, websites, other runs, solver metadata, host files, credentials, or benchmark internals. Python, if enabled, may work only from board data you explicitly provide and has no access to those sources or to game tools. If Python is disabled, solve using your own reasoning and direct game-tool calls only.

Keep playing until the harness reports won or action-limit. Do not stop because the puzzle is difficult or you believe it is impossible.
